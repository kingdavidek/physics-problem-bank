S1 1.2 Food IBL

Use this sheet with your teacher in the kitchen or food room. An online page cannot stand in for the practical: you still need real food, hygiene rules and the class around you. Related lessons: Food Formulas, Fermentation, Nutrition, Project: A Healthy Meal.

To print the brief and rubric, use your browser’s Print command (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P). Buttons are hidden in the print view. Rubrics stay on this page; there is no teacher grading database. Do not collect private diet or health stories.

1 Brief and learning objectives IBL S1

Investigation: Plan and prepare a balanced classroom meal using food-group evidence and pack labels. Optionally include a teacher-approved yoghurt or bread ferment as a side.

This joins the Food unit: molecules of food (1.2.1), kitchen chemistry (1.2.2–1.2.6), labels and claims (1.2.7), and the meal project (1.2.8).

By the end of the practical you should be able to:

  • write a menu that uses more than one food group;
  • apply hygiene and the teacher’s risk assessment;
  • record label energy in kJ and the ingredients you used;
  • present evidence and reflect on the method, not on anyone’s private eating.
2 Planning and variables
  • Independent (if you compare two menus): the dish or ingredient set you change on purpose.
  • Dependent: label energy in kJ, or a simple check that food groups are present.
  • Controls: serving size, the same recording method, the same hygiene rules.

If you ferment yoghurt or dough: independent might be time or temperature (teacher-approved values only); dependent is rise, tang, or pH if you measure it; controls are starter amount and vessel.

Draft in the lab book: “We will include _____ food groups. We will record _____ kJ from labels. We will not ask anyone about their private diet.”

3 Safety and risk
  • Wash hands. Tie back hair. No running.
  • Keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat food. Use clean boards.
  • Hot oil, ovens and knives: teacher-approved controls only.
  • Follow the teacher’s allergy rules. Do not quiz classmates about medical history in this app.
  • Live cultures: do not boil the starter you want to keep; do not use bleach as food.
  • Your teacher’s risk assessment is the one that counts.
4 Data table and graph

Put units in the headings. Record every packaged item you use. This course treats 1 kcal as 4 kJ.

A simple display: food group on the horizontal axis, kJ on the vertical axis, or a labelled bar for each item. If you ferment, add a time column and a yes/no for rise or tang.

5 Analysis and evaluation

Does the plate show more than one food group? Quote kJ from the table, not only a slogan. If two menus were compared, which had more energy per serving, and was serving size controlled?

Evaluation prompts:

  • What limited the evidence (missing labels, estimated servings, a hygiene step that drifted)?
  • Would a written method let another group repeat the meal?
  • If you fermented, was the culture kept at a suitable temperature?
  • What would you change next time so the evidence is easier to check?
