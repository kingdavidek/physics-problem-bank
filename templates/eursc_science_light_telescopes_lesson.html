2 Speed and the light-year

Light is very fast. This lesson uses the rounded figure 300000 km/s in vacuum. A light-year is the distance light travels in one year — not a unit of time like a minute, even though the name contains “year”. A distance, not a time. Light in vacuum travels about \(300000\,\mathrm{km}\) each second, so in one year it covers a huge distance called a light-year.

Worked example: In \(2\,\mathrm{s}\), light travels \(300000 \times 2 = 600000\,\mathrm{km}\). That is still much less than 1 AU (about \(150\) million km). A light-year is many AU, so it is a scale for stars, not for the Earth–Moon gap.

3 Shadows

An opaque object blocks light. Straight-line rays explain a simple shadow on a table or a wall. Darkness is not voted into place; it is where light does not arrive.

Worked example: A lamp, a book and a wall in a line: rays that miss the book reach the wall; rays that hit the book stop. The dark patch on the wall is the book's shadow.

4 Phases and eclipses

Moon phases are how much of the sunlit half of the Moon we can see from Earth. An eclipse needs a special alignment: for a solar eclipse the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and the Moon's shadow can fall on Earth. Not every New Moon is a solar eclipse for the whole Earth.

Worked sequence: Sun → Moon → Earth in a line (solar eclipse). Sun → Earth → Moon in a line (lunar eclipse). Everyday phases do not need a full eclipse.

6 Refraction

Refraction is a change of direction when light enters a different medium (for example air to glass or water). A change of direction when light goes into a new medium. It is not the same as bouncing from a mirror (reflection). Lenses use refraction in a controlled shape.

Worked example: A stick in a glass of water can look bent at the surface because rays from the underwater part change direction as they enter air. The stick itself is still straight.

7 Colour

White light is a mix of colours. A blue object in white light reflects blue and absorbs other colours. A green filter transmits green and absorbs other colours. Filters and surfaces do not create extra light from nothing.

8 Lenses and instruments

A lens gathers light and changes its direction so an image can be formed. A simple telescope uses lenses (or a mirror) to collect more light than the eye alone. Never look at the Sun through a telescope, binoculars or a lens — follow the teacher's solar-viewing rules. A lens does not invent extra light from fame.
