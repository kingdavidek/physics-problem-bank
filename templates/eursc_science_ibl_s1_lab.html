1 Brief and learning objectives IBL S1

Investigation: How does the temperature of water affect how long it takes a measured mass of sugar to dissolve?

This joins the three Science Lab lessons: you need public, testable evidence (1.1.1), SI units and a thermometer you can read (1.1.2), and a fair test with labelled apparatus (1.1.3).

By the end of the practical you should be able to:

  • write a testable question and name independent, dependent and control variables;
  • choose a thermometer and a stopwatch that match the size of the change;
  • record repeats with units in the table headings;
  • say what limited the accuracy and how the next trial would improve.
2 Planning and variables
  • Independent: water temperature (for example 20 °C, 40 °C, 60 °C — values your teacher approves).
  • Dependent: time for the sugar to disappear, in seconds.
  • Controls: mass of sugar, volume of water, stirring (same number of stirs, same spoon), same beaker size.

Change one independent variable at a time. Write a method another group could follow: quantities, instruments, how you start and stop the watch, and how many repeats.

Draft your question here in your lab book: “How does _____ affect _____ when _____ is kept the same?”

3 Safety and risk
  • Wear eye protection when heating water.
  • Tie back long hair. No running. Bags off the floor.
  • Point heating tubes and beakers away from people. Do not taste the mixture.
  • Hot water is a burn and spill hazard: carry beakers with dry hands, wipe spills at once.
  • Your teacher’s risk assessment is the one that counts. Add any extra hazard they name.
4 Data table and graph

Put units in the headings. Keep every raw reading, then calculate the mean. Do not delete an inconvenient value without saying so in the evaluation.

Temperature / °C Time 1 / s Time 2 / s Time 3 / s Mean time / s

A simple graph: independent variable on the horizontal axis (temperature), mean time on the vertical axis. Label both axes with quantity and unit. Plot the means; you may mark the range of the repeats as a short bar if your teacher asks.

5 Analysis and evaluation

Look at the pattern: as temperature increases, does mean dissolving time rise, fall, or stay similar? Quote numbers from the table, not only a feeling.

Was the result reproducible? Compare repeats at the same temperature. A large scatter means reaction time, uneven stirring or a poorly read scale.

Evaluation prompts:

  • What limited the accuracy (thermometer resolution, starting the watch late, a control that drifted)?
  • Would a mean of more repeats reduce random scatter?
  • Would a systematic offset (a clock that always reads high) need calibration instead of averaging?
  • What would you change in the next trial so another group could trust the evidence?
6 Teacher rubric

Local classroom judgement only. Circle one column per row. This is not stored in the app.

Criterion Developing Secure Strong
Method Steps missing or two variables change at once Followable method; one independent variable Quantities, instruments and repeats are explicit
Evidence Incomplete table or missing units Raw readings and a mean with units Repeats, mean and a graph that matches the table
Collaboration Roles unclear; one person does all the measuring Shared roles for timing, reading and recording Peers check the method and the scale reading
Communication Conclusion is a feeling with no numbers Pattern stated with units from the table Pattern, scatter and limits of the method are all said clearly
Reflection No comment on error or next steps Names one limit and one improvement Separates random scatter from systematic offset
