1 Forms 3.1.2 S3

This S3 set names example forms: kinetic (motion), chemical (food and fuels) and thermal (often the less useful leftover in a story). Kinetic is energy of motion. Chemical is energy stored in food and fuels. Thermal is energy linked to temperature; in many stories it is the less useful leftover.

2 Transformations

A transformation changes energy from one form to another, for example chemical to kinetic in a fictional bicycle story. Changing energy from one form to another. The energy is still there; it is not a new kind of stuff appearing from nowhere.

Worked example: In a classroom story, chemical energy in a fictional cyclist's snack is transformed into kinetic energy of the bicycle. Some of it is also transformed into thermal energy in the brakes later. No household bill is stored.

4 Losses

Some energy is wasted as a less useful form such as thermal. Conservation still holds: the energy has not vanished. Energy transferred into a less useful form, often thermal. Waste does not mean the energy has been destroyed.

5 Sankey-style split

A Sankey-style split shows an input splitting into useful and wasted parts. A input, B useful output, C wasted output. Schematic bars, not a measured home meter.

Worked example: A classroom lamp model takes 100 J in. 40 J is useful light. 60 J is wasted as thermal. Check: \(40 + 60 = 100\). The wasted 60 J has not vanished; it has been transferred into a less useful form. This is not a power calculation and not a home meter reading.

8 Conservation

Conservation means energy is not created or destroyed in this model. A machine does not make energy from nothing. Energy is not created or destroyed in this model. Useful and wasted parts still add up to the input.

In the Sankey example, 40 J useful + 60 J wasted = 100 J in. Conservation still holds.
