A simple S2 list for life as we know it: a source of energy (sunlight or chemical energy that can be tested), liquid water, and useful chemicals (for example carbon compounds). That is three items, not a rumour. Other worlds might surprise us; this course still starts from what we can measure on Earth.

2 Early Earth and LUCA

Conditions on early Earth are reconstructed from rocks, chemistry and models that other scientists can criticise. LUCA (last universal common ancestor) is a scientific model of shared ancestry from common chemistry — not a king in a history book and not a named celebrity. A model of a last universal common ancestor based on shared chemistry. It is evidence-based and open to better data, not a celebrity or a rumour. Life did not appear as a modern city in one day.

3 Life elsewhere and evidence

Whether life exists beyond Earth is an open scientific question. There is no confirmed evidence of life beyond Earth in this course. A claim must be testable with public measurements. A blurry photo of a light in the sky is not, by itself, evidence of microbes on Mars. A secret that cannot be checked is not scientific proof.

4 Travel and habitation

Other stars are so far that even light takes years (this lesson uses 4 years to the nearest other star as a rounded figure). Chemical rockets are much slower than light, so a trip of a few light-years is not an afternoon walk. A habitat beyond Earth must supply air, water, energy and radiation shielding — four engineering constraints. An airless world does not provide oxygen by rumour.
