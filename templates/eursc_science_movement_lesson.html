2 Average speed

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. It is not the fastest sprint in the middle of a race. If distance does not change, average speed is zero.

Worked example: A runner covers \(100\,\mathrm{m}\) in \(20\,\mathrm{s}\). Average speed \(= 100 \div 20 = 5\,\mathrm{m/s}\). A 2 s burst in the middle is not the average.

4 Unit conversion

Convert before dividing. \(2\) minutes \(= 120\,\mathrm{s}\). \(3\,\mathrm{km} = 3000\,\mathrm{m}\). Writing \(8\,\mathrm{km/h}\) as \(8\,\mathrm{m/s}\) without converting is a unit error: \(8\,\mathrm{m/s}\) is much faster.

5 Distance–time graphs

A sloping line: moving. B flat line: rest. C slope again: moving. A steeper slope means greater speed. Line colour and fame are not data.
