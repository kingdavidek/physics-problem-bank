Revolution is the planet's path around the Sun — its orbit. One revolution of Earth takes about one year. Orbiting the Sun. One revolution of Earth is about one year. Rotation (day) and revolution (year) are different motions. Rotation and revolution are different motions; a day is not a year.

3 Seasons and tilt

Earth's axis is tilted (about 23° in this course) as Earth orbits the Sun. A hemisphere in summer is tilted toward the Sun and receives more direct sunlight. A tiny change in Earth–Sun distance is not the main cause: northern summer is in July even though Earth is then slightly farther from the Sun.

Worked example: In July the northern hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, so days are longer and sunlight is more direct there. The southern hemisphere is in winter at the same time. Distance to the Sun is not the main switch.

4 The Moon

The Moon is a natural satellite that orbits Earth. We see it by sunlight reflected from its surface, not because it is a second star. Phases and eclipses are geometry: they belong with light in the next module.

5 Planets and other bodies

This course counts eight planets orbiting the Sun, plus moons, asteroids, comets and dwarf planets. They are not all the same size as the Sun. Order and composition differ; the shared idea is that they orbit the Sun (or orbit a planet, for moons).

6 Scale and the astronomical unit

An astronomical unit (AU) is the Earth–Sun distance. A Solar System distance scale: 1 AU is the Earth–Sun distance. This course uses about \(150\) million km as a rounded figure. On that scale the Earth–Moon distance is much smaller than 1 AU. Diagrams in books are almost never to scale: if the Sun were a large ball, Earth would be a small bead far away.

Worked example: 1 AU ≈ \(1.5 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{km}\). The Moon is about \(4 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{km}\) from Earth, so about \(0.003\,\mathrm{AU}\) — far smaller than the Earth–Sun gap. Jupiter is about 5 AU from the Sun in this teaching model.

7 Age and expansion of the universe

The universe in the current scientific model is ancient — of the order of 14 billion years in this lesson — and expanding. Public evidence includes distant galaxies receding. A telescope count does not make the universe a few thousand years old. Models stay open to better evidence.

8 Historical models and evidence

A geocentric model puts Earth at the centre. A heliocentric model puts the Sun at the centre with planets in orbit. Geocentric: Earth-centred. Heliocentric: Sun-centred. The older model was not kept because it was older — new evidence fitted a Sun-centred Solar System better. Authority is not the test; evidence is.
