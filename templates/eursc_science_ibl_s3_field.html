S3 3.2 Living Earth IBL

Use this sheet with your teacher. An online page cannot stand in for the practical: you still need the site, kit, the teacher's risk assessment and time to sample and record. 1 Brief and learning objectives IBL S3

Investigation: How does one chosen independent variable (for example shade, or distance from a path) affect a testable count (for example daisy plants per quadrat) at a site the teacher approves?

The field product is a classroom report judged with a rubric. This page does not auto-grade the product and does not store a league of whose plot is best.

By the end of the practical you should be able to:

  • write a testable field question and name independent, dependent and control variables;
  • follow the teacher's risk assessment and a sampling idea such as a quadrat;
  • record repeats with units in the table headings;
  • say what the numbers show and what you would change next time.
2 Planning and variables
  • Independent: one quantity the teacher approves (for example shade category, or distance from a path in metres).
  • Dependent: the count or cover in each quadrat (for example number of daisy plants).
  • Controls: same quadrat size, same counting rule, same day if the teacher asks, same way of placing the frame.

Change one independent variable at a time. Draft the question in the lab book: “How does _____ affect _____ when _____ is kept the same?”

3 Safety and risk
  • Stay on the site the teacher names. No private home-garden photo harvest into this app.
  • Follow the teacher's risk assessment: weather, traffic, plants that may irritate skin, and how far the group may walk.
  • Bags off paths. No running. Wash hands when the teacher says.
  • Do not pick protected plants unless the teacher has a permit and a reason. Counts can stay in place.
  • Your teacher’s risk assessment is the one that counts.
4 Data table and graph

Put units in the headings. Keep every raw value, then calculate the mean if you repeat the count. Do not delete an inconvenient value without saying so in the evaluation.

Independent variable (unit) Count, quadrat 1 Quadrat 2 Quadrat 3 Mean

A simple graph: independent variable on the horizontal axis, mean count on the vertical axis. Label both axes with quantity and unit.

5 Analysis and evaluation

Did the mean change with the independent variable? Quote numbers from the table. If a quadrat was empty, keep the zero; that is evidence, not a stored ranking.

Evaluation prompts:

  • Was the independent variable the only thing that changed?
  • Would more quadrats reduce random scatter?
  • What would you change so another group could trust the evidence?
  • How is a class rubric different from a popularity league in an app?
