6 Infra- and ultrasound, and tools

Infrasound is sound below typical human hearing. Ultrasound is above it. Infrasound: too low for typical human ears. Ultrasound: too high. Animals and technologies can use both. Animals and technologies can use both. Instruments extend what humans can detect; that is not a ranking of classmates as animals.

Worked example: A bat sends a high-pitched sound (often ultrasound). The echo returns from an insect. Time and direction of the echo locate the insect. A ship sonar uses the same returning-sound idea with a device.

7 Chemical senses and technology

Animals detect airborne or dissolved chemicals with smell and taste receptors. A device can do a similar job: a sensor that changes when a chemical is present. The shared idea is detecting a signal, not a superpower ranking.

Worked example: A moth antenna detects a chemical trail. A classroom carbon-monoxide alarm (a public safety device) also detects a chemical and produces a reading. Sequence: chemical (A) → sensor (B) → reading (C).

Keep UV, IR, polarisation, electric sensing, echolocation, infra/ultrasound and chemical sensing as separate adaptations and tools. S2 Senses ends here. This quiz does not rank pupils as animals.
